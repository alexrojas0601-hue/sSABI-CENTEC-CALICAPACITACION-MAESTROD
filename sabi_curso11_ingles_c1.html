C1 Academic English — Precision and Complexity
Nominalisation · Hedging · Complex syntax · Cohesion · Academic register
⏱ Sem 1-2
▽
C1 is the level where language stops being an obstacle and becomes an instrument of precision. At C1 you express complex ideas with nuance, write publication-quality texts, and participate in international academic discussions as an intellectual peer. This module targets the specific features that distinguish C1 from B2.
🧠
Nominalisation — Signature of academic English
Nominalisation converts verbs/adjectives into nouns, creating the formal dense style of academic writing. Examples: analyse becomes analysis, implement becomes implementation, significant becomes significance. Instead of: Teachers use technology more effectively when they are trained well. Academic C1: The effectiveness of technology use increases significantly with adequate teacher training. This creates information density, formal register, and objectivity.
💭 Rewrite 5 sentences from your professional context using nominalisation.
💡
Hedging — Academic caution and precision
Hedging signals claims are based on evidence, not absolute truth. Hedging language: modal verbs (may, might, could), adverbs (apparently, arguably, generally), verbs (suggest, indicate, appear, tend to). Strong claim: Technology improves learning. Hedged: Technology appears to improve certain aspects of learning under specific conditions. Hedging is not weakness — it is precision. Over-claiming undermines academic credibility more than under-claiming.
💭 Rewrite 3 strong claims using appropriate hedging language.
🎯
Complex Sentence Structures — C1 Syntax
C1 syntax uses: relative clauses (The methodology that we developed, which has since been adopted by three other schools), participle clauses (Having established the baseline data, we proceeded to), cleft sentences for emphasis (It was the formative feedback, not the summative assessment, that produced the improvement), and fronted adverbials (Under the right conditions, collaborative learning significantly outperforms individual study).
💭 Write 5 complex sentences about your teaching practice using these structures.
🔬
Cohesion — Making texts flow as connected wholes
Cohesion connects sentences into a unified argument. Tools: reference (this, these, such), substitution (This approach, like other student-centred methods...), conjunction (however, therefore, consequently, in addition, nevertheless), lexical chains (learning, acquisition, development, mastery all in one paragraph). The test: can a reader follow your argument without re-reading? If not, add cohesion.

C1 Academic Writing — Research and Publication
Literature review · Argumentation · Methodology · Discussion · Revision
⏱ Sem 3-4 🔒 Completa el anterior
▽
C1 academic writing produces texts that contribute to knowledge — not just demonstrate reading. This module develops: synthesising literature, constructing original arguments, describing methodology with precision, and writing discussions that go beyond results to implications.
🧠
Literature Review — Synthesis not summary
A literature review is not a list of what each author said — it is a structured argument using multiple sources as evidence. Techniques: thematic organisation (not chronological), synthesis moves (While Smith argues X, Jones and Chen suggest Y, which together indicate Z), critical engagement (these studies do not address the specific conditions present in Colombian secondary schools), and gap identification (No study to date has examined formative assessment in post-conflict educational contexts).
💭 Write a 200-word literature review paragraph synthesising at least 3 sources.
💡
Argumentation — Building the case
Academic argument structure (Toulmin model): Claim (what you argue), Grounds (evidence), Warrant (logical connection between grounds and claim), Qualifier (limitations), Rebuttal (anticipation of counter-arguments). Applied: Claim: formative assessment improves equity. Grounds: data showing smaller achievement gaps. Warrant: frequent feedback allows lower-performing students to course-correct before falling behind. Rebuttal: while some argue that formative assessment benefits higher performers most, this study's disaggregated data shows the opposite.
💭 Construct a complete Toulmin argument for one claim from your professional experience.
🎯
Methodology — Writing with precision
Methodology writing elements: participant description (who and how many), context specificity (where and when), data collection procedures (what and how), analytical approach (how you interpreted), limitations acknowledgment. Passive voice is standard: Data were collected over two semesters. Interviews were conducted with 12 participants. Thematic analysis was employed. Precision principle: another researcher should be able to replicate your study from your methodology description alone.
💭 Write a 150-word methodology description for an investigation you have conducted or could conduct.
🔬
Discussion — Beyond results to implications
Discussion elements: interpretive claim (what results mean), connection to literature (how your findings relate to existing knowledge), alternative interpretations (what else could explain the results), practical implications (what practitioners should do differently), and research implications (what should be studied next). Common error: restating results in the discussion. Results = what happened. Discussion = why it matters, what mechanism explains it, what it means for theory and practice.

Facilitating Complex Professional Discussions
C1 facilitation language: opening (I would like to invite you to consider...), redirecting (That is a valuable point — connecting it to what Maria said earlier...), synthesising (It seems the group is converging on the idea that...), managing disagreement (I notice we have two distinct perspectives here — what do they share?), closing (Before we conclude, let us articulate the key commitments we are taking away). The facilitator shapes the quality of collective thinking without dominating it.

Mentoring in English — Coaching language at C1
Mentoring language: diagnostic (What do you see as the primary challenge in that situation?), exploratory (What would need to be true for that approach to succeed?), challenge (I wonder if there might be an assumption worth examining there), affirmation (The precision with which you articulated that concern is exactly the clarity we need), and action (What is the one thing you will do differently based on our conversation?). Mentoring in English is adapting your style to English pragmatic conventions — not just translating it.

The Colombian communication style — warm, relational, and concrete — is a genuine asset in intercultural leadership when combined with C1 precision.
