Grammar Upgrade B1 — Complex Tenses and Structures
Present Perfect · Past Perfect · Conditionals · Passive Voice · Reported Speech
⏱ Sem 1-2
▽
At B1 level you move from survival English to professional English. You can describe past experiences, express hypothetical situations, report what others said, and use passive structures typical of academic writing. This module builds the grammar foundation that separates functional English from professional English.
🧠
Present Perfect vs Past Simple — The distinction that confuses most learners
Past Simple: completed action with specific time marker. I visited London in 2019. Present Perfect: connection between past and present — no specific time. I have visited London (so I know it). Time markers: Past Simple uses yesterday, last year, in 2019, when I was young. Present Perfect uses already, yet, just, ever, never, since, for, recently. Test: can you add a specific time? If yes, use past simple. If no, use present perfect.
💭 Describe 3 professional experiences using present perfect (no time) and 3 using past simple (with time).
💡
Conditionals — Real and Hypothetical
Zero conditional: always true facts. If water reaches 100 degrees, it boils. First conditional: real future possibility. If I improve my English, I will get better opportunities. Second conditional: hypothetical present/future. If I had more resources, I would design better materials. Third conditional: hypothetical past. If I had studied English earlier, I would have been promoted sooner. For Colombian teachers: second conditional is perfect for professional reflection: If I had smaller classes, I would...
💭 Write one conditional sentence for each type about your teaching situation.
🎯
Passive Voice — Essential for academic and professional writing
Form: be + past participle. The curriculum is designed by the Ministry. Students are evaluated every semester. The program was implemented last year. Use passive when: the action is more important than the agent, the agent is unknown, or you want formal/impersonal tone. Active: Teachers design the curriculum. Passive: The curriculum is designed by teachers. Academic writing and official documents favor passive.
💭 Rewrite 3 active sentences from your professional context in passive voice.
🔬
Reported Speech — Saying what others said
Direct: She said 'I teach mathematics.' Reported: She said that she taught mathematics. Tense shift rules: present simple becomes past simple. Present continuous becomes past continuous. Will becomes would. Can becomes could. Must becomes had to. Reporting verbs: said, told, explained, mentioned, reported, announced, argued, claimed, suggested. Key difference: said (no object) vs told (with object): She said she was tired. She TOLD ME she was tired.

B1 Skills — Speaking, Writing, Listening, Reading
Four skills integration · Argumentation · Academic writing · Listening strategies
⏱ Sem 3-4 🔒 Completa el anterior
▽
B1 is the threshold level: you can handle most situations in English-speaking environments related to your professional field. This module develops the four skills simultaneously — because real language use integrates all of them.
🧠
Speaking at B1 — Structured argumentation
B1 speaking: express opinions with reasons, describe experiences in detail, handle most professional conversations. Structuring arguments: State your position (In my view... / I believe that...), give reason (This is because...), give example (For instance, in my classroom...), concede opposing view (Although some might argue...), restate position (Nevertheless, I maintain that...). Practice: record yourself arguing for and against a pedagogical position for 2 minutes without stopping.
💭 What is your position on standardized testing? Argue it in English for 90 seconds.
💡
Writing at B1 — Professional texts
B1 writing: produce clear structured text on topics in your field. Academic essay structure: Introduction (thesis statement), Body paragraphs (topic sentence + evidence + analysis), Conclusion (restate thesis + implications). Linking words: However (contrast), Furthermore (addition), Therefore (result), In contrast (comparison), Consequently (result), Nevertheless (concession). Colombian teachers' advantage: you have rich professional experiences to write about — the content is yours. The form is learnable.
💭 Write a 200-word argument for or against one teaching methodology you use.
🎯
Listening at B1 — Strategic comprehension
B1 listening: understand the main points of clear speech on familiar topics. Strategies: prediction (use context to anticipate content), listening for gist (first listening: what is the general topic?), listening for detail (second listening: specific information), inference (what do the speaker's words imply?). For Colombian teachers: start with educational content (TED-Ed, English language teaching podcasts, Coursera videos on education) where you already know the topic — easier to process.
💭 Watch one 5-minute educational video in English. Note: main topic, 3 key points, 1 thing you did not understand.
🔬
Reading at B1 — Academic and professional texts
B1 reading: understand the main content of texts on topics related to your interests. Strategy: skimming (read quickly for the main idea), scanning (search for specific information), intensive reading (careful reading for full understanding). For academic articles: read the abstract + conclusion first, then headings, then return for detail in relevant sections. The author's argument is usually in the introduction. The evidence is in the body. The implications are in the conclusion.

Negotiating in Professional Contexts
Negotiating is not arguing — it is finding mutually acceptable solutions. Language for negotiation: Making a proposal: I would like to suggest... What if we tried...? Would it be possible to...? Responding to proposals: That sounds reasonable, however... I can see your point, but... I am not sure that would work because... Finding middle ground: Could we perhaps compromise on...? What if we started with... and then evaluated? Professional negotiation signals maturity and collaborative problem-solving — key skills for school leadership.

Presenting Research and Innovation
Structure for a 5-minute professional presentation: Hook (30 sec), Context and Problem (60 sec), Your Approach or Solution (90 sec), Evidence and Results (60 sec), Implications and Call to Action (60 sec). Transitions that signal structure: I would like to start by... Moving on to... This brings me to... To summarize... My recommendation is... Visual support: limit text on slides — use data, images, and key words only. The slide supports — the speaker explains.

Intercultural Communication
English is now a lingua franca — most international English communication happens between non-native speakers. This changes the goals: clarity and comprehension matter more than native-like pronunciation. Research (Jenkins 2024): the features of English that most frequently cause miscommunication between non-native speakers are: overly complex vocabulary, unclear references (this, that, it), and speed. Solutions: speak clearly (not slowly), use precise nouns (not pronouns), and confirm understanding explicitly.

I can describe experiences, events, dreams, hopes and ambitions. I can briefly give reasons and explanations for opinions and plans. 🟢 What is the most important thing to maintain during the B1 to B2 journey to avoid plateauing?
✅ ¡Correcto!
✅ The B1 plateau is one of the most documented phenomena in language learning. Learners reach a level where they can communicate effectively in their usual contexts and lose the discomfort that drives growth. C1 academic writing: clear, detailed text on complex subjects, synthesizing and evaluating information from multiple sources. For publications: argument-driven structure (not topic-driven), evidence synthesis (not evidence listing), critical voice (your interpretation, not just reporting), and disciplinary conventions (using the language your field expects). The Path to C2 — When Language Disappears
