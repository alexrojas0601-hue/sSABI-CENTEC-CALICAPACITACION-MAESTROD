Alphabet · Greetings · Numbers · Present Simple · Basic vocabulary
⏱ Sem 1-2
▽

🧠
The Alphabet and Sounds
English has 26 letters but 44 phonemes. The hardest for Spanish speakers: TH (think=/th/, this=/dh/), W vs V (wine is not vine), the schwa sound /e/ in unstressed syllables. Trick: English spelling does not always match pronunciation. Practice: say the alphabet out loud every day until automatic.

Greetings and Introductions
Hello / Hi / Good morning / Good afternoon / Good evening / Good night. My name is... / I am... / Nice to meet you / How are you? Key cultural note: Good night is a FAREWELL in English, not a greeting. How are you? expects a short answer: I am fine, thanks. Not a medical report!

Present Simple — The most used tense
Form: I/You/We/They + verb. He/She/It + verb+s. I work, She works. Negative: I do not work, She does not work. Question: Do you work? Does she work? Use: routines and permanent facts. I teach at CENTEC (fact). She speaks English (ability). Key rule: He/She/It always adds S.

Numbers, Colors, Days and Months
Numbers 1-100. Colors: red, blue, green, yellow, black, white, orange, purple. Days: Monday through Sunday (they end in -day). Months: January through December. Memory trick: the days in English are named after planets and Norse gods (Saturn-day, Sun-day, Moon-day).

Presente Continuo, Can y Vocabulario del Aula
Present continuous · Can/Cannot · Classroom language · There is/are
⏱ Sem 3-4 🔒 Completa el anterior
▽
📚 Como maestro colombiano que quiere usar inglés en clase, hay un vocabulario prioritario que necesitas antes que cualquier otro: el inglés del aula. Open your books. Listen carefully. Work in pairs. Estas frases, bien pronunciadas, transforman tu clase en inmersion parcial sin necesitar ser completamente fluido todavia.
🧠
Present Continuous — What is happening now
Form: am/is/are + verb + ing. I am teaching. She is reading. They are working. Spelling rules: come is coming (drop E). run is running (double final consonant). play is playing (keep Y). Use: actions in progress RIGHT NOW. I am writing on the board (at this moment). Contrast: I teach English (routine/present simple). I am teaching English right now (in progress/present continuous).
💭 Look around you. Write 3 sentences about what people near you are doing right now.
💡
Can and Cannot — Expressing ability
Can + base verb (no TO). I can speak three languages. She cannot swim. Can you drive? Can is INVARIABLE — never add S. She CAN, never she cans. Can has two uses: ability (Can you sing?) and informal permission (Can I use your pen?). For formal permission use May I...? For polite requests use Could I...?
💭 Write 5 things you can do and 3 things you cannot do as a teacher.
🎯
Classroom Language — The English you need today
Commands (imperative): Open your books. Close the door. Listen carefully. Work in pairs. Raise your hand. Write your name. Instructions: Please turn to page... Look at the board. Repeat after me. Questions to students: Do you understand? Any questions? What does this word mean? Can you give an example? Student responses: I do not understand. Can you repeat please? I think the answer is...
💭 Choose 5 phrases and commit to using them in English in your next class.
🔬
There is and There are — Describing what exists
There is + singular: There is a whiteboard in my classroom. There is not a projector. Is there a computer? There are + plural: There are 35 students. There are not any books. Are there any pencils? Common error: There is many students — WRONG. Many signals plural: There ARE many students. One item: is. More than one: are.

Pasado Simple y Vocabulario Profesional
Past simple · Regular/Irregular verbs · Professional vocabulary · CV language
⏱ Sem 5-6 🔒 Completa el anterior
▽
📖 El pasado simple es el tiempo verbal mas usado en ingles para contar lo que paso. Las experiencias que defines como maestro, los logros en una hoja de vida, las historias que cuentas en el aula — todo ocurre en pasado simple. Este modulo te da las herramientas para contar tu historia profesional en ingles.
🧠
Past Simple — Regular Verbs
Regular verbs: base + ED. work is worked, learn is learned, start is started, study is studied (y changes to ied), plan is planned (double final consonant). Negative: DID + NOT + BASE verb. I did not work yesterday. Question: DID + subject + BASE verb? Did you study English last night? IMPORTANT: In negatives and questions, the main verb returns to BASE form. Did you worked? WRONG. Did you work? CORRECT.

💡
Past Simple — Irregular Verbs
Essential irregular verbs for teachers: go is went, have is had, make is made, say is said, know is knew, think is thought, come is came, take is took, see is saw, give is gave, teach is taught, find is found, tell is told, leave is left, feel is felt, begin is began, bring is brought, write is wrote, read is read (pronunciation changes), understand is understood. Memory tip: group by pattern (bring/think/buy all become brought/thought/bought).

Teaching: curriculum, assessment, rubric, feedback, engagement, methodology, learning outcome, differentiation, scaffolding, formative, summative, portfolio. Administration: enrollment, attendance, transcript, grade, semester, syllabus, faculty, staff, policy. Action verbs: collaborate, coordinate, implement, evaluate, facilitate, mentor, develop, design, analyze, improve.

Futuro y Comunicacion Profesional en Ingles
Will · Going to · Would · Email writing · Presentations · Professional English
⏱ Sem 7-8 🔒 Completa el anterior
▽
El 67% de las oportunidades de formacion docente internacional publicadas en Colombia requieren al menos comunicarse por escrito en ingles (MEN 2024). Escribir un email profesional, participar en una conferencia virtual, presentar tu trabajo en un foro internacional — estas no son habilidades del futuro. Son habilidades del presente.
🧠
Will vs Going to — Two futures with different intentions
WILL: spontaneous decisions, promises, general predictions. I will help you with that. It will rain tomorrow. I promise I will send the report. GOING TO: plans already made, predictions based on visible evidence. I am going to redesign my curriculum next semester (already planned). It is going to rain — look at those clouds (visible evidence). Trick: Going to means I already thought about it. Will means I decide right now.
💭 Write 3 professional plans using going to and 3 spontaneous offers using will.
💡
Would — The conditional that makes everything more polite
Would makes English more professional and polite. Would you like...? More polite than Do you want? I would like to request... More formal than I want. For professional emails: I would appreciate if you could... I would like to schedule a meeting to discuss... For conditions: If I had more time, I would develop more materials.
💭 Rewrite these more politely: Send me the report. I want a meeting. Can you help?
🎯
Professional Email Writing — The structure that works
Subject: Clear and specific. Request for Professional Development Leave March 15-17. Opening: Dear Dr. or Mr. or Ms. Last name for formal. Hello First name for informal colleagues. Body: State purpose, add details, make request or describe next steps. Closing: Best regards or Kind regards or Sincerely + Full name + Title + Institution. Key phrases: I am writing to... I would like to... Please find attached... I look forward to hearing from you.

🔬
Presentations and Meetings — Professional English phrases
Starting a presentation: Good morning everyone. Today I am going to talk about... My presentation covers three main points... Transitioning: Moving on to... As I mentioned earlier... To summarize... In meetings: I would like to add... Could you clarify...? I agree with... I see your point but... What do you think about...? Ending: Thank you for your attention. Are there any questions?

Reading Academic English — Strategies to not get lost
Academic texts in English have predictable structure: Abstract, Introduction, Methods, Results, Discussion, Conclusion. Strategy SQ3R: Survey (scan the text), Question (convert headings into questions), Read (to answer your questions), Recite (summarize in your own words), Review (check your summary). Academic vocabulary: however, therefore, furthermore, in contrast, consequently, as a result, in conclusion. With these tools you can extract 80% of the value of any English article.

🟢 Which sentence correctly uses the Present Perfect?
✅ ¡Correcto!
✅ I have taught at CENTEC since 2015 — present perfect: have or has + past participle. Taught is the past participle of teach. Since + specific point in time = present perfect because the action started in the past and continues now. Option A: have teach needs past participle: taught. Option C: been teacher should be been A teacher. Option D: taught since 2015 — since with past simple is wrong. Since always requires present perfect.
💭 Write 3 sentences about your career using present perfect with since and for.
🔄 Reflexiona de nuevo
Present perfect + since: I have taught since 2015. Teach is irregular: teach becomes taught. Since + present perfect for ongoing situations that started in the past.

🟠 Which strategy is most effective for reading a complex English education article?
✅ ¡Correcto!
✅ SQ3R is the most research-validated academic reading strategy. Key insight: you do not need to understand every word to extract main ideas. The abstract plus introduction plus conclusion plus headings give you 80% of the content. Reading every word sequentially is inefficient for academic texts. Avoiding difficult texts eliminates access to the best professional development resources. Difficulty with the right strategy becomes manageable and then comfortable.
